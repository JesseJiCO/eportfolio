About me

I grew up in Gaithersburg, Maryland, and have been immersed in mathematics and problem-solving from a young age. I attended the Mathematics, Science, and Computer Science Magnet program at Montgomery Blair High School, where my introductory physics and algorithms & data structures classes sparked my interest in physics and computer science.

By my sophomore and junior year, I was entering math and computing competitions like USACO and ACSL, and I'd interned at three different physics labs at the Johns Hopkins University and the University of Maryland by the summer after my junior year. I discovered a strong passion for research and using computational tools to understand complex physical and biological systems.

Despite being admitted to Georgia Tech as a physics major, my career goals and aspirations led me to pivot to computer engineering, where I could build on my foundational math and science knowledge and apply them to engineering challenges. Now, I'm focused on gaining the engineering skills and experience to contribute meaningfully to this field.

More projects

Beller Lab, JHU Bloomberg Center for Physics & Astronomy (Baltimore, MD, June–August 2025). As a paid research intern, I developed a Python data-analysis pipeline using KD-trees and graph-based clustering to quantify emergent patterns in active matter simulations of cytoskeletal filaments.

I analyzed spatial correlations and cluster dynamics across model parameters and identified a novel active state in which filaments form but destabilize nematic lanes. I presented the findings at the JHU REAL Undergraduate Showcase and the APS Southeastern Section Annual Meeting.

Porto Lab, UMD Department of Physics (College Park, MD, June–August 2025). As a research intern, I engineered the first computational prototype for a quantum optics simulation framework in Python, modeling complex wave propagation for proposed optical setups.

I identified performance bottlenecks and tested several optimization approaches. The results were incorporated into a graduate researcher's PhD candidacy proposal.

Sternbach Lab, UMD Department of Physics (College Park, MD, November 2023–September 2024). As a research intern, I built a database of nonlinear optical properties of organic crystals to support quantum materials research.

I also used LabVIEW to investigate near-field and far-field imaging in atomic force microscopy (AFM) experiments.
